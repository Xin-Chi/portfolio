Capstone: Artificial Intelligence–Based Healthcare-Associated Bloodstream Infection Surveillance and Classification System

Sep 2021 – Feb 2023
First place in the university capstone competition, presented internationally at ISQua's 39th Poster Display; the system is deployed and in use by the infection control department
  • Ran repeated requirements interviews with infection control nurses to turn clinical judgment criteria into system rules
  • Restructured the data model from event-centric to patient-centric and built the MSSQL infection control database
  • Used n-grams to extract key information from unstructured text such as nursing records and lab reports
  • Built a rule-based decision support system that cut case review from 2–3 weeks to 1 day, removing 97% of manual re-checking
  • Developed the web case-collection system, with AI acting in an advisory role and a nurse making the final call
  • Built a hospital-wide infection dashboard with Power BI

Live web demo →

Data Modeling: From Event-Centric to Patient-Centric
The hospital's original data was stored by medical event — labs in one place, nursing records in another — so a nurse had to move across several subsystems just to piece together one patient's picture. Redesigning around a patient-centric model meant a single patient lookup returns every related medical event, making search and filtering far faster.
Requirements Interviews: Turning Clinical Criteria into System Rules
Clinical workflow and system logic don't line up on their own, so I interviewed infection control nurses repeatedly to pin down what the criteria actually meant. Even something as simple-sounding as “length of stay” had to be settled: subtract the dates, or count it out to the hour? Getting that wrong is how a system's verdict drifts away from what clinicians expect.
